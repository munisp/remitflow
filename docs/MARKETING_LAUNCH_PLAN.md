## Executive Summary

RemitFlow enters a global remittance market valued at **USD 188.93 billion in 2025**, projected to reach USD 381.98 billion by 2031 at a CAGR of approximately 12.4% [^1]. Digital remittances alone are growing from USD 28.9 billion in 2025 [^2], driven by smartphone penetration, diaspora growth, and the decline of cash-based transfer agents. The cross-border payments market more broadly is projected to grow from USD 397.37 billion in 2026 to USD 727.74 billion by 2034 [^3].

This plan outlines a 90-day sprint to sign the first 100 white-label partners onto the RemitFlow platform, followed by a 12-month retention and growth programme. The strategy is built on three pillars: **targeted outreach to regulated fintechs and MFIs**, **community-led growth through diaspora networks**, and **content authority in the remittance compliance space**.

## 1. Market Opportunity & Partner Landscape

### 1.1 Total Addressable Market

The white-label remittance platform market sits at the intersection of two high-growth segments: the remittance infrastructure market (technology providers to money transfer operators) and the embedded finance market. Globally, there are an estimated **2,500–4,000 licensed money service businesses (MSBs)** in the United States alone, with comparable numbers across the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia [^4]. In Africa, the number of licensed mobile money operators and payment service providers exceeds 1,200 across 45 countries [^5].

[^4]: [FinCEN — MSB Registrant Search Database](https://www.fincen.gov/msb-registrant-search)
[^5]: [GSMA — State of the Industry Report on Mobile Money 2025](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/gsma_resources/state-of-the-industry-report-on-mobile-money/)

### 1.2 Ideal Partner Profiles

RemitFlow's white-label programme is designed for five distinct partner archetypes, each with different motivations and acquisition channels:

| Partner Type | Description | Primary Motivation | Estimated Market Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diaspora Fintech** | Startups serving specific diaspora communities (Nigerian, Kenyan, Ghanaian, Indian) | Speed to market; avoid building infrastructure | 500+ globally |
| **Microfinance Institution (MFI)** | Licensed MFIs adding remittance as a product line | New revenue stream; client retention | 1,200+ in Africa/Asia |
| **Community Bank** | Small regional banks serving immigrant communities | Compete with Western Union/MoneyGram | 800+ in US/EU |
| **Mobile Money Operator** | Telco-backed mobile money services expanding internationally | International corridors; diaspora flows | 300+ in Africa |
| **Remittance Agent Network** | Physical agent networks seeking digital upgrade | Digital transformation; lower operating costs | 2,000+ globally |

### 1.3 Competitive Positioning

RemitFlow differentiates from competing white-label platforms on three dimensions:

| Dimension | RemitFlow | Typical Competitor |
|---|---|---|
| **Time to launch** | 20 minutes (self-service wizard) | 4–12 weeks (manual onboarding) |
| **Compliance coverage** | Built-in AML, KYC Tier 1–3, sanctions screening | Varies; often requires third-party integration |
| **Pricing** | Revenue-share model; no upfront licence fee | $50K–$200K upfront licence + annual maintenance |
| **Corridors** | 40+ corridors at launch | Typically 10–20 |
| **BNPL for transfers** | Included | Rare; typically not available |

## 4. Partner Acquisition Funnel

### 4.1 Funnel Stages

| Stage | Definition | Target Conversion Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Visited website or saw an ad | — |
| **Interest** | Visited `/partner/onboard` or requested demo | 5% of awareness |
| **Consideration** | Started onboarding wizard (entered invite code) | 40% of interest |
| **Activation** | Completed onboarding (launched platform) | 70% of consideration |
| **Retention** | Active partner at 30 days (≥1 transaction) | 80% of activation |

### 4.2 Conversion Optimisation

**Reducing friction at the invite-code gate:** The invite-code requirement is a deliberate quality filter, but it must not create unnecessary friction for genuinely interested prospects. The following measures ensure qualified prospects can always get a code:

- A "Request an Invite Code" form is prominently displayed on the `/partner/onboard` page for visitors without a code
- Codes requested via the form are issued within 4 business hours
- A "Partner Hotline" phone number is displayed for urgent requests

**Reducing drop-off in the onboarding wizard:** The 6-step wizard auto-saves progress at every step, so prospects can return and complete at their own pace. An automated email sequence re-engages prospects who abandon the wizard:

- **1 hour after abandonment:** "Your RemitFlow platform is almost ready — continue where you left off"
- **24 hours after abandonment:** "Your invite code expires in 6 days — don't miss your spot"
- **5 days after abandonment:** "Final reminder: your invite code expires tomorrow"

## 9. Competitive Response Plan

As RemitFlow gains traction, established competitors (Currencycloud, Thunes, Nium) may respond with aggressive pricing or accelerated feature development. The following defensive strategies protect the partner base:

**Lock-in through integration depth:** The deeper a partner integrates RemitFlow's API into their product, the higher the switching cost. Encourage partners to use the full feature set (BNPL, recurring payments, agent network) rather than just basic transfers.

**Community moat:** The partner community becomes a self-reinforcing asset. Partners who share best practices, refer new partners, and co-create case studies have a stake in the platform's success.

**Compliance advantage:** RemitFlow's built-in compliance engine is difficult for competitors to replicate quickly. Continuously invest in compliance features (new jurisdiction support, enhanced AML models) to maintain this lead.

**Speed of innovation:** Ship new features faster than competitors. The 20-minute onboarding is a product advantage today; maintain it by continuing to invest in developer experience and self-service tooling.
